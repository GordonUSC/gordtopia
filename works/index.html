The Nether Spine

ending word: gate

The motto is All Roads Lead Home. There is no road. This is the only job on the board that makes every other job cheaper.

31 of 40 · 78% of the ceiling

Ninety landmarks are spread across 15,822 blocks east to west. Sprinting from the bell out to all four extremes and nowhere else is 29,686 blocks, about eighty eight minutes of holding a key down. The same four runs through the Nether are 3,711 blocks, about eleven. The eight to one ratio is not a trick and it is not new. It is just that Gordtopia has never spent it.

Done looks like
You can stand at the bell and be at any of the four edges of the world in under fifteen minutes, with no boat and no sprinting.

Watch for
Bedrock will happily link a new portal to any existing one within 128 blocks on the Nether side. Build both ends by hand at exact coordinates, or two of your four doors will end up fighting over the same portal and you will not notice until you are a thousand blocks out.

3 third

The Map Wall, at its true extent

ending word: hold

The wall in the plan is five by five. A five by five wall covers 10,240 blocks. The world is 15,822. The wall you planned is smaller than the nation you built.

26 of 40 · 65% of the ceiling

A max zoom map covers 2,048 blocks square. To hold a world 15,822 wide and 13,505 tall you need eight tiles across and seven down, which is 56 maps, and if the game’s own tile lattice falls badly you need nine by eight, which is 72. Either way the five by five was drawn for a smaller Gordtopia than the one that exists now. This is the same thing that happened to Plate I in August, and it is a good problem: it is what a world outgrowing its paperwork looks like.

The steps

  1. Sugarcane before anything else. A single max zoom tile is one empty locator map plus four zoom outs, and the wall is 56 of them. Build the farm first or you will stall halfway through the second row.
    per tile    8 paper + 1 compass, then 4 × 8 paper  =  40 paper
56 tiles    2,240 paper  ·  56 compasses  =  224 iron, 56 redstone
  2. Find the lattice before you build the frames. Make one max zoom map standing at the bell, walk to its corners and read them. The game aligns these tiles on its own fixed grid, so let that first tile define the wall rather than deciding the grid on paper.
    target grid   8 across × 7 down,  2,048 blocks a tile
must contain  X -7893 .. 7929    Z -5091 .. 8414
  3. Build the frames eight wide and seven tall, and hang the tiles you have. Leave the frame empty everywhere you have not been.
  4. A map only fills where a player has actually stood. The blank squares are not missing pieces, they are the frontier, drawn to scale, and they are the only honest answer to where have we not been.
  5. Give each brother a column. Filling a column is a real job with a visible end, which is rare in a world this size.

Done looks like
A wall you can walk up to and point at, where every filled square is somewhere the family actually walked and every blank one is an invitation.

The good part
This is the only object in Gordtopia that measures the walking instead of describing it. The atlas, the plates and the survey all tell you where the world is. The wall is the only one that can be wrong in your favour, by showing you have covered more than you thought.

The Deep Run, and a compass for the wolf

ending word: deep

Ninety landmarks and every one of them is on the surface. The way underneath is already on your map, at the southernmost point in the world.

23 of 40 · 57% of the ceiling

The register holds no fortress, no bastion, no stronghold, no ancient city and no End. Gordtopia is a purely overworld nation, which is a true and rather beautiful fact about it and also a ceiling. And the door is already logged: Southern Bones sits in deep dark at Y minus fifty, which is ancient city depth exactly. Then there is Wolfmark, entered on 22 August with the line a wolf fell from a high place, an event the world should keep. Echo shards come out of ancient cities. Eight of them and a compass make a recovery compass, which points at the last place something died. There is a version of this trip that ends with a compass in a frame at Wolfmark.

The steps

  1. Go in from the south end of the spine. Southern Bones is already the far anchor of job one’s south door, which is why this sits below the road and not above it.
    Southern Bones  82    -93, -50, 8414   deep dark
Wolfmark        84    544, 69, 2440   the loss
  2. Carry wool and place it under you before you drop, jump or break anything near a shrieker. Wool eats vibration, and vibration is the only sense down there that matters.
  3. Do not break a sculk shrieker unless you have decided to. It warns before it calls, and the fourth shriek is the one that brings the thing that hunts you.
  4. Take the echo shards, the Swift Sneak books and whatever the redstone room gives up. Swift Sneak is the single best thing in the game for a world where you sneak past danger rather than fight it.
  5. Craft the compass and frame it at Wolfmark. It will not point at the wolf. It will point at wherever you last died, which is a different and in some ways better memorial: a marker that keeps updating.
    recovery compass  =  8 echo shards + 1 compass
  6. Log the city. It becomes Landmark 91 and it is the first entry in the register that is not on the surface of the world.

Done looks like
The register stops being a surface only document, and the wolf gets a marker that points at something instead of a line of text.

The call to make first
This is the one item on the board with a monster that hunts by sound and is not meant to be fought. James is ten, the twins are eight. Either it is the trip you take alone and bring home to them, or it is the trip you take together with the difficulty stated plainly at the top. Both are good answers. It is a much better decision to make at the surface than at the bottom of the hole.

Light the Sleeping Gate pinned

ending word: gate

It scores near the top under all three lenses. It sits last anyway, because it cannot happen before the road exists.

24 of 40 · 60% of the ceiling

Sleepgate is a gold crowned obsidian arch standing unlit over water, and the doctrine was written into the record months ago: an unlit portal is not unfinished, it has chosen its moment. That is the reason this is last and it is not a demotion. The moment is when there is a road for it to be a door on. Its Nether coordinate sits almost exactly on the west spur of job one, so if you run that corridor you will pass within sight of the far side of a gate that has never been opened.

  2. Build the Nether side frame there by hand while you are digging the corridor, and leave it dark. Passing an unlit frame on the way west is a good thing to have in a tunnel.

  4. Change one field in the register afterwards. Sleepgate stops being an unlit obsidian arch and becomes a door, and that is the first time in the whole survey that a landmark has changed what it is rather than what it is called.

Done looks like
Nothing measurable. A thing that has stood dark since before the survey existed stops being dark, on a night everyone was there for.

Why it is pinned
The model would rank this second or third depending on the lens. It is held at five by a hard dependency rather than by its score, and the board shows you that rather than quietly burying it. If you ever build the west door and stop, come back and light this anyway.
